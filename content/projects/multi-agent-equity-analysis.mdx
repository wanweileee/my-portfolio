## Why I'm building this

I read about multi-agent trading systems a lot — the kind that break an investment decision into specialised reasoning steps instead of asking one model to handle the whole thing. After enough reading, I wanted to try one myself.

There is a second, more honest motivation: I am also learning how to trade. Writing the system out is the fastest way to force myself to understand what a real investment decision actually requires. You cannot write a Fundamentals Quality sub-agent without first deciding what "quality" *means* to you — and that decision turns out to be most of the work.

This is very much a work in progress.

> **Status**: In progress · **Stack**: Python, LangGraph, yfinance, SEC EDGAR

## What I'm building

A multi-agent system that analyses US equities and produces structured investment recommendations — *action, conviction, position size, entry, stop, and target* — by combining technical analysis, fundamentals research, and risk assessment under one orchestrated pipeline.

The system runs three top-level agents in parallel:

- **Price Coordinator** — trend, momentum, patterns, volume
- **Fundamentals Coordinator** — six specialist sub-agents covering valuation, quality, growth, financial health, capital allocation, and moat
- **Risk Agent** — volatility, drawdown, position sizing

A **Synthesis Agent** aggregates their outputs into a single decision with explicit veto and gating logic.

LangGraph orchestrates the whole workflow as subgraphs with shared state — parallel execution, conditional routing, and a clean upgrade path from deterministic Python nodes to LLM-powered ones.

## Design choices worth highlighting

**Specialisation over generalism.** Splitting Fundamentals into six sub-agents (rather than one monolithic agent) makes reasoning auditable — when a call goes wrong, I can trace which sub-agent led the system astray. Same logic for splitting the Price agent into Trend, Momentum, Pattern, and Volume sub-agents, mapped to John Murphy's *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets* framework.

**Hard vetoes and soft gates.** The Financial Health sub-agent can veto the entire recommendation if solvency metrics flag danger. The Trend sub-agent can gate the action to HOLD even on strong fundamentals if all timeframes are in downtrend. These prevent the two most common beginner failure modes: buying cheap dying companies, and catching falling knives.

**Deterministic now, LLM-ready later.** Every agent is a pure Python function today, but the graph structure and state schema are designed so LLM upgrades are single-node swaps. Highest-priority upgrade targets: Synthesis (narrative thesis writing), Moat (10-K text reasoning), and a future Red-Team Agent.

## What I'm learning

Two things in parallel — how to architect a system where the *seams* between agents carry as much meaning as the agents themselves, and how to read a balance sheet without flinching. The build is forcing me to put words to ideas I have been reading about for months. That alone has been worth it.

## Reading alongside

I am currently working through John J. Murphy's *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets* — the textbook the Price sub-agents are modelled on. Trend, momentum, pattern, and volume each get their own chapter, and the agent structure mirrors that almost one-to-one. Easier to design the seams when the source is open on the desk.
