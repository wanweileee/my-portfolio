PokéPets is a small brand identity experiment — a fictional product at the intersection of collectible-card aesthetic and pet-care marketing. The full set of visuals was generated using image models, treating each one as a separate brief: a logo to anchor the identity, a hero image to set the tone, and supporting visuals to fill out a hypothetical campaign.

## What I'm learning

Generating a coherent brand system — not just one good image, but a *set* that hangs together across logo, hero, and supporting cuts — turns out to be more about prompt scaffolding than prompt cleverness. The model is much happier shipping variations of a defined visual language than inventing one from scratch. The work I spent on the *style brief* (palette, line weight, composition rules) compounded across every subsequent image. The work I spent perfecting any single prompt did not.

The other thing this exercise made clear: a logo is the easiest single image to *generate* and the hardest single image to *evaluate*. Two pieces of supporting marketing tell you whether the system can flex. One logo on its own tells you almost nothing.
